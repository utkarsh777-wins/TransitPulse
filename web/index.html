    Likelyarrival, not a cumulative interval

    A timetable promises one minute. The road rarely keeps it. TransitPulse learns how each stretch of NH-44 really behaves, then tells riders the range their bus will most likely arrive in, how sure it is, and when to leave.

    How it works

    From a GPS ping to a window you can trust.

    Five steps, each one inspectable. Bad pings are dropped before they reach the model, and the window is a separate model, not a fudge factor on the point estimate.

    See the full pipeline →
    1. 01

      GPS telemetry

      Position, speed and stop dwell for every trip on the corridor.

      4,800 pings · 30 days

    2. 02

      Quality gates

      Geofence, speed sanity, dwell imputation and duplicate removal.

      4 gates · 97.81% kept

    3. 03

      Delay model

      Random-forest regressor and classifier over 12 features.

      RF · days 1–24 train

    4. 04

      Window

      10th and 90th percentile models set the range. Peak hours widen it.

      q10 / q90 · ×1.35 at peak

    5. 05

      Rider guidance

      Likely arrival, status, and a leave-by time.

      leave_by_min from POST /api/simulate

    Built for Punjab

    For the rider at the stop and the depot behind the route.

    Tier-2 towns and intercity highways have long headways, mixed traffic and patchy data. A late bus can cost a rider half an hour. The answer is an honest range, not a falsely precise number.

    Riders

    Plan around a range.

    • An arrival window and a leave-by time for your stop from /api/simulate
    • A plain notice when peak traffic or rain makes the window wider
    • Never told "board now" when the model is unsure
    Operators & evaluators

    See why a segment is late.

    • Per-segment delay, split into 3-trip lag, peak, weather and dwell
    • Data-quality audit and SHA-256 checks on every model artifact
    • Designed for AIS-140 GPS feeds (on the roadmap)
