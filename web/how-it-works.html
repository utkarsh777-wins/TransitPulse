How it works

From a GPS ping to a window you can trust.

Every arrival window passes through the same five steps. Each one can be inspected in the evaluator view, from rows dropped at the gates to the SHA-256 of the model file.

The five steps

  1. 01

    GPS telemetry

    Position, speed and dwell for each trip and segment.

    4,800 pings · 30 days · generated

  2. 02

    Data-quality gates

    Four gates drop or repair bad telemetry before training.

    4,800 → 4,695 rows

  3. 03

    Delay model

    A regressor predicts minutes of delay; a classifier cross-checks the status.

    RandomForest × 2 · 12 features

  4. 04

    Calibrated window

    Quantile models give the 10th and 90th percentile; the window is centred on the point delay.

    GradientBoosting α 0.10 / 0.90

  5. 05

    Rider guidance

    10th–90th range, status, leave-by time, and a plain-language reason.

    served by POST /api/simulate

Calibrated window

Two gradient-boosting quantile models estimate the 10th and 90th percentile of delay. The published window is centred on the point prediction, has a minimum half-width of 0.6 min, and is widened by 35% in peak hours (08–10 and 17–19).

# src/app.py: interval centred on the point delay
half_spread = max(0.6, (raw_upper - raw_lower) / 2.0)
half_spread *= 1.35 if is_peak_hour else 1.0
lower = round(max(-1.0, delay - half_spread), 1)
upper = round(delay + half_spread, 1)

Rider guidance

Status comes straight from the point delay. Riders get the 10th–90th range and a leave-by time. A wide range is not a coverage percentage, and the advice does not say to board now when the bus is late.

Leave-by is the window start minus walk time. POST /api/simulate accepts walk_time_min (default 5) and returns leave_by_min on every segment. The live demo has a walk-time slider.
